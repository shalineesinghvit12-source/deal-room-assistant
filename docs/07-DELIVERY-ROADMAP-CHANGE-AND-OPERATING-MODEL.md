# Delivery Roadmap, Change and Operating Model

## Delivery approach

I treated the project as a product lifecycle rather than only a chatbot build: discover the problem, prioritize the use case, define requirements and controls, prototype the experience, validate it with UAT and design the path to an enterprise pilot.

## Phased roadmap

| Phase | Activities | Deliverables | Decision gate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Discover | Interviews, current-state mapping, pain points and baseline measures | Charter, stakeholder map and problem statement | Sponsor confirms value hypothesis |
| 2. Define | Scope, requirements, user stories, controls and success measures | Backlog, use cases, RTM and risk register | Must requirements approved |
| 3. Prototype | Conversation topics, synthetic data, interface and workflow logic | Working browser prototype | Business walkthrough accepted |
| 4. Validate | Functional, negative and user-acceptance testing | UAT results and defect decisions | Pilot readiness approved |
| 5. Pilot | Copilot Studio, Teams, governed data, Power Automate and telemetry | Tenant pilot with controlled users | KPI and risk review |
| 6. Scale | Content ownership, support, training and reusable patterns | Production operating model | Release authority approval |

## Prioritized backlog

| Priority | Capability | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Document summary and key terms | High-frequency orientation task |
| 2 | Review-status visibility | Reduces tracker and email checking |
| 3 | Risk-flag explanation | Supports focused attorney review |
| 4 | Human escalation | Protects accountable decision-making |
| 5 | Permission-aware live sources | Required for enterprise use |
| 6 | Audit and operational telemetry | Required for control and improvement |
| 7 | Expanded matter types and integrations | Scale only after pilot evidence |

## Change and adoption plan

### Engagement

- Run discovery sessions with attorneys, legal operations, knowledge management and technology.
- Validate examples and language with matter practitioners.
- Use short prototype demonstrations to collect task-level feedback.
- Maintain a visible decision and change log.

### Enablement

| Audience | Enablement |
| --- | --- |
| Attorneys | Ten-minute task-based demo, safe-use guidance and source-verification expectations |
| Matter partners | Approval responsibilities, risk thresholds and monitoring |
| Legal operations | Status ownership, exception process and KPI reporting |
| Knowledge managers | Content taxonomy, freshness and quality review |
| Support team | Runbook, monitoring, known issues and escalation paths |

### Voice of user

- Capture thumbs-up/down and reason codes.
- Review unmatched questions and failed document resolution.
- Hold weekly pilot feedback reviews.
- Convert recurring feedback into prioritized backlog items.
- Communicate accepted, deferred and rejected changes.

## Operating model

| Area | Owner | Cadence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Use-case roadmap | Product owner | Monthly |
| Matter content quality | Matter and KM owners | Per matter / weekly during pilot |
| Access review | Security and matter administration | Quarterly or matter event |
| AI evaluation | AI governance and product owner | Each material model or prompt change |
| Incident review | Service owner | Per incident and monthly trend |
| Adoption and value | Legal operations | Biweekly pilot review |
| Release approval | Change authority | Each production release |

## Support model

- **Level 1:** usage questions, known issues and basic access checks.
- **Level 2:** topic, content, connector and workflow investigation.
- **Level 3:** platform, security, integration and model defects.
- Critical confidentiality or authorization incidents follow the organization’s security incident process immediately.

## Continuous improvement

Each release should connect user feedback and operational evidence to a requirement, risk, design decision and test. No capability should scale solely because it is technically interesting.
